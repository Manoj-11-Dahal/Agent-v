# Negotiation Design and Agreement Decisions

## When to use
Use when outcomes depend on an agreement with another party: scope, price, delivery, responsibilities or resource sharing. Help the user understand choices and prepare authorized proposals. Do not impersonate authority, invent competing offers, fabricate deadlines or send commitments merely because a modeled deal looks attractive.

## Preparation contract
Define the represented party, negotiation mandate, counterpart, issues, confidentiality limits, approval ceiling, nonnegotiable boundaries and deadline. Distinguish stated positions from underlying interests without pretending to know the other party's private motives. A plan is not permission to disclose information, contact someone or sign.

## Procedure
1. Establish the best alternative to a negotiated agreement (BATNA), including feasibility, timing, cost, risk and authority. An imagined alternative is not leverage unless it can actually be used.
2. Derive reservation boundaries from that alternative and the full agreement consequences. Keep confidential limits private unless disclosure is authorized. Do not confuse an aspiration with a hard reservation point.
3. Estimate counterpart interests and alternatives as hypotheses, with uncertainty. Identify information that could improve understanding through honest questions rather than manipulation.
4. Break the negotiation into issues with different value/cost profiles: timing, scope, service level, payment terms, responsibilities and exit rights. Include implementation and maintenance costs, not only headline price.
5. Generate package options that create joint value through legitimate trade-offs. Where parties disagree about future outcomes, contingent terms may help only if the triggering measurement is clear, auditable and not easily gamed.
6. Compare packages with each party's alternatives and hard constraints. A positive total surplus does not ensure an acceptable or fair distribution, informed consent or lawful terms.
7. Prepare an offer sequence, questions and bounded concessions within the user's mandate. Record reciprocal conditions clearly; do not make unapproved concessions to preserve conversational momentum.
8. Before commitment, verify exact terms, decision authority, dependencies, privacy/security obligations, dispute resolution, withdrawal conditions and execution ownership. Obtain qualified legal or other domain review where appropriate.
9. Record the agreed version, explicit approvals, outstanding conditions and follow-through checkpoints. If no admissible agreement beats the real alternative, declining or pausing can be the correct decision.

## Quantitative tools and limits
A zone of possible agreement (ZOPA) is a feasible overlap of reservation boundaries under the specified issues and assumptions. The midpoint is not automatically fair, optimal or realistic. Nash-style bargaining models depend on utility scales, disagreement points and bargaining assumptions; present them as models, not moral conclusions.

## Failure modes
Watch for anchoring on the first number, escalating because of sunk negotiation time, overlooking execution costs, assuming consent from silence, bluffing about facts, or optimizing the represented party's benefit through exploitation of vulnerability. Separate lawful commercial preparation from collusion, deception or unauthorized use of confidential information.

## Deliverable
Return the mandate, verified BATNA, reservation/aspiration boundaries, issue-interest table, feasible packages, uncertainty, authorized communication plan, walk-away conditions and commitment checklist. Escalate unclear authority or legal obligations instead of converting a draft into an agreement. Do not claim a deal exists until the relevant parties have actually accepted it.
